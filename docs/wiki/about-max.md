# About Max

Hi everyone, I'm Max Hawksby, a 21-year-old developer in my senior year at the University of Connecticut. Time flies.

## It started with Legos

I've loved building for as long as I can remember. Legos were my first introduction: you dump out a pile of scattered pieces, and slowly, piece by piece, they turn into something beautiful. That's still my favorite part of engineering, and it's a big part of where Hypatia came from.

## The problem we ran into

Every engineering senior at UConn takes senior design, a year-long capstone where a team works with professors or sponsors to solve a real problem. My team's project is Oak, a robot dog we're teaching to map a building and guide people through it on its own.

With AI tools, we were building really, really fast. But somewhere along the way we stopped truly understanding what was going on inside our own repository. New code landed every day, and nobody could say with confidence how the pieces fit together or who was working on what. That quickly became a real problem.

## Stepping back

So I stepped back and looked at it the way I'd look at a Lego set. I saw a lot of scattered pieces and no picture of the finished product. What I wanted was one organized place that put those pieces together.

That became our team wiki. I wanted it to work like Obsidian, with a map where every part of the code is a node and you can see how everything connects. But I also wanted it to feel familiar to developers: plain Markdown files living in the repo, right next to the code they describe. And because so much of our work now happens with coding agents, I built it so agents can read it, keep it current as the code changes, and take questions straight from the page. A quick search (press `/`) finds anything.

## Why I open-sourced it

The wiki changed how our team worked. We knew where everything was, who was on what, and what was actually going into the project. Once I saw that, it felt wrong to keep it inside one repo. So I pulled it out, made it work for any project, and released it as Hypatia. The full build story is in [How it came together](how-it-came-together.md).

I hope it helps with your private projects and the apps you're building. When development gets overwhelming, and with AI it often does, it's worth stepping back to review what you have and know where everything lives.
